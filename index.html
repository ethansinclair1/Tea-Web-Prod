How to brew

Instructions

The same basic method works for every tea here — just adjust water temperature, leaf amount, and steep time to what's listed on each card.

  1. 01

    Heat your water

    Use the temperature listed for your tea — off the boil for delicate teas, fully boiling for robust ones.

  2. 02

    Measure your leaf

    Use the leaf amount listed per card, usually per 250ml (about one mug) of water.

  3. 03

    Steep

    Time it to the steep time listed — the ring on each card's photo shows this at a glance.

  4. 04

    Strain and enjoy

    Remove the leaves so the tea doesn't turn bitter, then drink while it's hot.

How much tea do I use per cup?

Each tea card lists a leaf amount for one cup (about 250ml). A heaped teaspoon is roughly 2–3g for most of our teas. Use a bit more if you like it stronger rather than steeping for longer, which tends to make it bitter.

Can I re-steep the leaves?

Yes, most of them. Oolong and pu-erh are made for it and often get better on the second or third go. Add 30 seconds to a minute to each extra steep. Black tea and herbal blends usually give one good cup.

How should I store loose leaf tea?

Keep it sealed, somewhere cool and dark, away from strong smells like coffee or spices. The pouches it comes in are resealable, so they're fine to keep it in. Green and white teas taste best within about 6 months of opening.

What do the dots under the price mean?

That's the caffeine level — three dots is about as strong as tea gets, no dots means it's caffeine-free. Use the Caffeine-free filter to see only those.

Why small batch

We buy less, so we can taste all of it first.

Zita's Teas orders in small lots rather than blended commodity stock. That means a bag can sell out and not come back until the next harvest — we'd rather tell you that plainly than pad the shelf with something we wouldn't steep ourselves.

Every tea on this page is one we drink at home. If a lot doesn't taste right, it doesn't go up for sale.
